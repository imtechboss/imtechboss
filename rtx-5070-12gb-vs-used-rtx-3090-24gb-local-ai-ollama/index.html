Direct Engineering Verdict: If your workflow is strictly limited to 7B and 8B parameter models (such as Llama-3.3-8B or Qwen-2.5-7B) or FP4/FP8 image generation, the NVIDIA GeForce RTX 5070 (12GB GDDR7) delivers superior energy efficiency (250W TDP), native Blackwell FP4 Tensor Cores, and full warranty coverage at $549. However, for serious local AI engineering—including 14B, 32B, and 70B parameter LLMs, long-context RAG pipelines (32k+ tokens), and LoRA fine-tuning—the used GeForce RTX 3090 (24GB GDDR6X) (~$620–$680) remains the undisputed champion because VRAM capacity and memory bandwidth (936 GB/s vs. 672 GB/s) dictate LLM performance far more than raw compute FLOPS.

1. Why Local LLM Inference Is Memory-Bound, Not Compute-Bound

When building a local AI workstation for tools like Ollama, LM Studio, llama.cpp, or vLLM, the single biggest mistake PC builders make is applying gaming GPU logic to Large Language Model inference. In rasterized gaming or ray tracing, a newer architecture with faster clock speeds and architectural IPC gains (such as NVIDIA Blackwell over Ampere) easily outperforms a two-generation-older flagship.

Autoregressive token generation in transformer models works differently. Generating each new token requires reading the entire active model weight matrix from GPU VRAM into the streaming multiprocessors (SMs) for every single forward pass. Unless you are running massive batched requests on an enterprise server, single-user token generation speed (measured in tokens per second, or t/s) is governed almost entirely by two physical hardware limits:

  • Total Dedicated VRAM Capacity (GB): Determines whether the model weights and the Key-Value (KV) context cache fit entirely inside high-speed GPU memory. The moment a model spills over by even 500 MB into system DDR5 RAM over the PCIe bus, inference speeds collapse by 75% to 90%.
  • Memory Subsystem Bandwidth (GB/s): Dictates how rapidly the GPU can stream quantized weights from VRAM into the Tensor Cores for each generated token.

Why the 32B Parameter Tier Is the Decisive Battleground in 2026

In 2026, the sweet spot for local autonomous coding agents (such as Roo Code, Cline, and Continue.dev) and reasoning models (such as DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-32B) sits squarely at the 24B to 32B parameter tier. While 8B models are fast, they frequently hallucinate complex multi-file refactoring logic. A 32B model quantized to Q4_K_M occupies roughly 18.5 GB for raw weights alone, plus 2.0 GB to 3.5 GB for the KV context cache.

On the 12GB RTX 5070, you are forced to offload nearly half of a 32B model's transformer layers onto system DDR5 RAM. Because dual-channel DDR5-6000 only delivers ~85 GB/s of real-world throughput, your inference speed plummets below 10 tokens per second—slower than human reading speed. On the 24GB RTX 3090, the entire 32B model plus a 16,384-token context window fits inside GDDR6X memory, cruising at over 41 tokens per second.

4. Where the RTX 5070 (12GB) Actually Beats the Used RTX 3090

Despite the 12GB VRAM limitation, there are three specific workloads where the newer Blackwell architecture in the RTX 5070 outperforms the aging Ampere flagship:

  1. Native FP4 & FP8 Diffusion Workloads (Flux.1 & Video Generation): Blackwell's 5th-generation Tensor Cores support hardware-accelerated FP4 and FP8 micro-scaling formats. When running Flux.1-dev or local diffusion pipelines quantized to FP8/FP4 that fit within 12GB, the RTX 5070 generates images 22% faster than the RTX 3090 while consuming 120W less power.
  2. Prompt Processing (Time-to-First-Token): When feeding a massive 6,000-word document into an 8B model for summarization, the initial prompt evaluation phase is heavily compute-bound. The RTX 5070's higher Tensor FLOPS and architectural improvements process initial prompts roughly 15% to 20% faster than the RTX 3090, provided the model fits in 12GB.
  3. Thermal Acoustics & Power Supply Compatibility: A used RTX 3090 has 24 GDDR6X memory chips mounted on both sides of the PCB. The backplate memory modules routinely reach 95°C to 104°C under sustained AI loads unless re-padded, and transient power spikes require at least an 850W ATX 3.0/3.1 PSU. The RTX 5070 sips 250W and runs whisper-quiet on a standard 650W power supply.

5. Pre-Purchase Inspection Checklist for a Used RTX 3090

If you choose the used RTX 3090 route for its 24GB VRAM, never buy blind. Run these diagnostic checks immediately upon installation:

# 1. Verify PCIe Link Width & Clock State under load (Linux / WSL2)
nvidia-smi -q -d PERFORMANCE,POWER,TEMPERATURE,CLOCK

# 2. Run a sustained 15-minute VRAM allocation & bandwidth stress test in Ollama
ollama run qwen2.5-coder:32b-instruct-q4_K_M --verbose

On Windows, open HWiNFO64 (Sensors-Only Mode) during a continuous LLM generation loop and inspect the GPU Memory Junction Temperature. If the memory junction exceeds 98°C while the GPU core stays at 70°C, the factory thermal pads on the backplate have dried out. Replacing them with 1.5mm or 2.0mm 12.8 W/mK Gelid Extreme or Honeywell PTM7950 pads takes 30 minutes and drops GDDR6X temperatures by 14°C to 18°C.

6. Power Efficiency Tuning: Undervolting the RTX 3090 for 24/7 AI Servers

One of the biggest hidden advantages of local LLM inference is that memory bandwidth stays at 100% even if you lock the GPU core clock down. By running a single terminal command, you can cap your RTX 3090's power limit from 350W down to 240W while losing less than 3% of your token generation speed:

# Enable persistent driver mode and cap RTX 3090 power draw to 240 Watts
sudo nvidia-smi -pm 1
sudo nvidia-smi -pl 240

At 240W, the RTX 3090 matches the power draw of the RTX 5070 while retaining double the VRAM (24GB vs. 12GB) and 39% higher memory bandwidth.

Frequently Asked Questions (Technical Deep-Dive)

Can I combine two used RTX 3090 GPUs for 48GB of VRAM in Ollama and vLLM?

Yes. Two RTX 3090 GPUs provide 48GB of combined GDDR6X VRAM, allowing you to run Llama-3.3-70B at Q4_K_M quantization with a 32,768-token context window completely in GPU memory. Furthermore, the RTX 3090 is the last consumer GeForce card to support physical NVLink bridges, which dramatically speeds up tensor parallelism in vLLM and multi-GPU PyTorch training.

Does PCIe 5.0 on the RTX 5070 make CPU offloading fast enough to replace 24GB VRAM?

No. While PCIe 5.0 x16 doubles the interconnect bandwidth between the CPU and GPU to ~64 GB/s unidirectional, your system RAM itself (dual-channel DDR5) remains capped around 85–95 GB/s. When layers are offloaded to system RAM, the CPU processes those layers at DDR5 speeds rather than GDDR7 speeds (672 GB/s), causing a severe bottleneck regardless of PCIe generation.

How do I enable Flash Attention and KV Cache Quantization in Ollama to save VRAM on a 12GB RTX 5070?

Set the environment variables OLLAMA_FLASH_ATTENTION=1 and OLLAMA_KV_CACHE_TYPE=q8_0 (or q4_0) in your system profile before launching the Ollama server. Quantizing the KV cache from FP16 to Q8_0 cuts context memory usage in half with virtually zero perceptual loss in output accuracy, letting a 12GB RTX 5070 push a 14B model up to a 16k context window.

Frequently Asked Questions & Technical Clarifications

Can an RTX 5070 12GB run 32B parameter models using CPU offloading? ▼

Yes, but with a severe speed penalty. Inference runners like Ollama will offload roughly 12 to 14 layers to system DDR4/DDR5 RAM, cutting generation speeds from 42 tokens/sec down to 4 to 6 tokens/sec over the PCIe bus.

What power supply is required when running a used RTX 3090 for local AI? ▼

A high-quality 850W or 1000W ATX 3.0 power supply is strongly recommended. The RTX 3090 draws 350W sustained under full compute loads and produces microsecond transient power spikes exceeding 500W that can trip older power supply protections.

Does the RTX 5070 support FP4 precision in Ollama? ▼

Yes. NVIDIA Blackwell architectures include dedicated FP4 Tensor Cores. When inference engines like vLLM and llama.cpp implement native FP4 GEMM kernels, 14B models can run within 8.5 GB of VRAM at unprecedented speed.
