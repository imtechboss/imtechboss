AMD's Lisa Su Touches Down in Seoul for Critical Samsung HBM4 Summit

AMD Chief Executive Officer Dr. Lisa Su is scheduled to hold a high-stakes bilateral summit in Seoul on October 8, 2026, with the upper echelon of Samsung's leadership, reportedly including Chairman Lee Jae-yong. The subject of this high-level meeting is the lifeblood of modern artificial intelligence hardware: High Bandwidth Memory, specifically the next-generation HBM4 standard. AMD is moving aggressively to finalize supply volumes and pricing structures for its upcoming 2nm CDNA 5-based Instinct MI455X accelerators.

This summit represents a critical juncture in the AI hardware race. While compute architectures have advanced rapidly, the industry is facing a severe bottleneck. Getting data into and out of the massive GPU cores fast enough to keep them fed is the primary limiting factor in training and running advanced neural networks. HBM4 is the solution, and securing enough of it is a matter of corporate survival.

The Memory Bottleneck in AI Superclusters

The artificial intelligence hardware sector is no longer purely constrained by compute capabilities; it is gated by memory bandwidth. Massive language models and agentic architectures require terabytes of data to be processed in milliseconds. Standard memory simply cannot move data fast enough, resulting in idle compute cores waiting for information. High Bandwidth Memory utilizes 3D stacking and Through-Silicon Vias (TSVs) to place memory dies directly adjacent to the compute die, providing immense bandwidth.

However, manufacturing HBM is incredibly complex, with low yields and highly constrained production capacity globally. Demand is currently outstripping supply by orders of magnitude, causing prices to skyrocket and forcing tech giants to secure allocations years in advance.

Inside the Instinct MI455X and CDNA 5

AMD's upcoming Instinct MI455X accelerators are based on the cutting-edge CDNA 5 architecture, manufactured on a 2nm process. These chips are designed specifically for hyperscale AI workloads, featuring massive matrix multiplication engines and enormous thermal envelopes. To unleash the full potential of CDNA 5, AMD requires the extreme bandwidth and power efficiency promised by HBM4.

The MI455X is projected to utilize advanced CoWoS (Chip-on-Wafer-on-Substrate) packaging to integrate multiple CDNA 5 compute tiles with surrounding HBM4 stacks. This complex multi-chip module design requires absolute perfection in both the logic dies and the memory stacks; a failure in any single component renders the entire multi-thousand-dollar package useless.

Helios Rack-Scale Solutions for Anthropic

The urgency of Dr. Su's mission is driven by massive customer commitments. AMD is preparing to deliver its Helios rack-scale superclusters to major hyperscalers, with Anthropic reportedly heavily invested in the architecture for training future models beyond Claude Sonnet 5.5. The Helios architecture connects hundreds of MI455X accelerators via advanced networking fabrics, creating a unified computing behemoth.

To meet the delivery timelines for these multi-billion-dollar Helios deployments, AMD cannot suffer any supply chain interruptions. The HBM4 supply must flow uninterrupted, and currently, the traditional supply chains are under immense pressure.

SK Hynix Strains and TSMC Packaging

Historically, SK Hynix has dominated the HBM market, capturing the vast majority of orders for NVIDIA's Hopper and Blackwell AI accelerators. This immense demand from NVIDIA has severely strained SK Hynix's production capacity. Furthermore, TSMC's advanced packaging facilities, which assemble the memory and compute dies, are also operating at maximum capacity.

By engaging directly with Samsung, AMD is executing a strategic diversification. Samsung possesses massive memory fabrication capabilities and has heavily invested in HBM4 research and development. If Samsung can guarantee the volume and technical specifications required for the MI455X, AMD can secure a steady flow of memory outside of the SK Hynix bottleneck, potentially gaining an edge in time-to-market for its Helios clusters.

The High Stakes of the Seoul Summit

The October 8 summit is not merely a vendor-client meeting; it is a strategic alignment. Dr. Lisa Su must negotiate terms that ensure AMD receives top-tier allocation of Samsung's finest HBM4 dies. In return, Samsung secures a massive, guaranteed revenue stream and a vital anchor client to validate its HBM4 technology against SK Hynix. The outcome of this meeting will dictate the production timeline of the MI455X and directly impact the balance of power in the generative AI hardware market over the next two years.
